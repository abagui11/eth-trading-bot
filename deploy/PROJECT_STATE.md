## 1. What this system is

A Telegram bot that runs an hourly dual-asset LLM trading cycle and a sub-hourly dual-asset programmatic watchdog over Coinbase ETH-USD and BTC-USD data. W1 ETH/BTC relative strength biases asset selection and soft-gates watchdog entries. Every suggestion is validated and audited before broadcast, then applied to a **house/agent paper book** (public journal). Subscribers hold **separate personal demo accounts**; trade DMs include a decision chart plus Accept/Reject — only Accept (or a later missed-connection Join) puts that user's cash into the trade. Personal ledgers are on `/me` (Telegram magic link). State is persisted to SQLite and surfaced through a FastAPI dashboard and Telegram read-back.

Four operator-facing paths (hourly, watchdog, Telegram chat/inline UI, dashboard), one shared data/context layer, one house paper book, and per-user personal books.

## 3. Data + market context layer

`research.py` pulls ETH-USD and BTC-USD feeds from Coinbase; `patterns/market_context.py` → `build_market_context` assembles one `MarketContext` per product. `patterns/relative_strength.py` aligns weekly ETH and BTC bars into an ETH/BTC ratio, detects nearby W1 zones/SFPs, and infers `eth_strong`, `btc_strong`, or `neutral`. The hourly proposal receives that context as an asset preference; the watchdog rejects only entries that clearly fight it (long weaker asset or short stronger asset), so it remains a soft gate rather than a standalone signal.

Live strategy timeframes: **H4 → H1 → M5**. H12 remains available for research/historical studies only.

### Tester pool (`pool.py`) — pooled live allocations, per-user accounting

Inert until `POOL_ENABLED`. 10-20 approved testers fund the one house Coinbase
account (~$1,000 each) and join live trades with real money:

- **Access is admin-gated.** With the pool on, an unknown user's first message
  files an `approved_users` request and DMs every pool admin an **Admit/Deny**
  card. Admit opens a zero-balance `pool_accounts` row, DMs the welcome, and
  mints a one-time invite link to the private forum group.
- **Hybrid UX.** With the pool on, **trade cards are personal DMs** so each
  card can show that tester's Accept risk
  (`POOL_RISK_PCT` of available cash) and approximate position size at the
  stop. Research/z-move pushes can still use the forum Research topic when
  configured. Account traffic (portfolio, deposits, fill/exit notices) stays
  in DM (`/portfolio`, `/deposit`; `/me` aliases to `/portfolio` for pool
  users). Onboarding and deposit copy state plainly that sizes are
  intentionally small while the strategy is still being solidified.
- **Registered wallets and return-to-source.** `/wallet 0x…` binds the address
  a tester funds from (`pool_wallets`), and it is required before `/deposit`.
  It does two jobs: attribution (an arriving transfer is matched by its
  **sender**, not by what someone typed) and payout destination — withdrawals
  return there and nowhere else, so client funds can only go back to an
  address whose control has been demonstrated. A first registration is
  self-serve (an empty account has nothing to redirect); a **change** needs an
  admin tap and then holds payouts for `POOL_WALLET_COOLDOWN_HOURS`, because
  re-pointing the payout address is an account takeover's first move and the
  delay is the window in which the real owner can object. Addresses are
  normalized lowercase with one live address per account and one account per
  address — two testers sharing one address would make sender attribution
  ambiguous, which is the whole point of holding it. **No EIP-55 checksum
  check:** there is no keccak-256 in this environment, and hand-rolling one in
  a money path risks rejecting valid addresses or blessing invalid ones, to
  guard a typo class that copy-paste makes rare. The property that matters is
  proven more strongly downstream — see verification below.
- **A wallet is `pending` until the chain says the deposit came from it.**
  `verified` is reached only through `pool.verify_wallets_onchain`, which
  compares the registered address against the **actual sender** of a credited
  deposit read from Etherscan (`chain.verify_deposit`). The admin's Credit tap
  does **not** set it, because the tap means funds were seen arriving, not
  that their origin was seen. `pool.payout_target` is the single place that
  decides where a withdrawal may go, and it refuses `unverified` and
  in-cooldown addresses. A mismatch still credits — the money is in the
  account and is the tester's — but leaves the wallet unproven and blocks
  payouts, with both the tester and an admin told why.
- **Chain reads (`chain.py`) answer the two questions Coinbase won't.**
  Read-only, holds no key that could move funds, and used for exactly two
  jobs: **who sent a deposit** (the sender Coinbase omits, which is what makes
  return-to-source a guarantee rather than a promise) and **whether a payout
  landed** (the status Coinbase will not return). Both come from Etherscan v2
  `account/tokentx` filtered to the USDC contract at the API — so a worthless
  token airdropped to the deposit address cannot be mistaken for a deposit,
  the classic way a naive watcher credits somebody for nothing. The USDC
  contract address is **pinned, not configurable**: a wrong value there would
  verify against a token nobody sent, and an attacker-issued token is
  trivially mintable. `deploy/_verify_chain.py` validates the reads against
  the real transfers already on the deposit address, because fixtures we wrote
  cannot catch a wrong contract address.
- **"We could not check" and "it was not them" are kept distinct**, and this
  is the invariant the design rests on. A lookup failure returns
  `lookup_failed` and is retried; only `verified`, `mismatch` and
  `wrong_destination` are terminal (`pool._TERMINAL_CHECKS`). Collapsing the
  two would mean an Etherscan outage reads as a failed proof and locks an
  honest tester out of their own money. Every attempt is recorded in
  `pool_wallet_checks` (unique per proof/claim pair, updated in place) so a
  retry refines the verdict, a mismatch is raised once rather than every
  minute, and the sender we actually observed is on the record.
- **Where the sender is read from matters.** `chain._from_receipt` decodes the
  USDC `Transfer` **event**, not the transaction's `from` field: if a tester
  funds from a smart-contract wallet or a multisig, `tx.from` is a relayer and
  the real token sender only appears in the log. Verifying against `tx.from`
  would refuse honest users and could attribute a transfer to the wrong one.
  Deposits also need `chain.MIN_CONFIRMATIONS` (12) before they prove
  anything, deep enough that a reorg undoing a credited deposit is not a
  practical concern on mainnet.
- **Payout settlement is confirmed at the destination.** `get_transaction`
  404s even with transfer scope, so our own balance dropping is the only venue
  evidence — and that proves money left, not where it went.
  `watchdog._settle_sweep` matches an inbound transfer at the tester's address
  on amount **and a time floor after submission**, then marks the withdrawal
  `settled` and DMs the tester the transaction hash. The time floor is
  load-bearing: without it an earlier withdrawal of the same size would mark
  this one settled and hide a payout that never arrived.
- **Deposits land at the venue and credit themselves.** `POOL_DEPOSIT_ADDRESS`
  is the Coinbase USDC deposit address, so transfers become venue equity on
  arrival — no intermediate wallet, no sweep step, no hot signing key, and no
  overlap with the yield sleeve's wallet. `/deposit 1000 <txid>` files a
  `pool_deposit_requests` row; the watchdog's 60s `_deposit_sweep` then reads
  settled inbound transfers (`DerivGateway.get_inbound_transfers`) and
  `pool.observe_chain_deposits` credits any whose hash matches a filed claim,
  DMing the tester **within a minute of settlement** with no admin in the
  loop. Admins get an FYI, not a gate. `/credit`, `/debit` and the Credit card
  remain as escape hatches; `pool.pending_inbound_usd()` is
  claimed-but-uncredited venue cash, i.e. the slice of apparent house residual
  that is really a tester's.
- **Attribution is by transaction hash, because Coinbase gives us no sender.**
  `/api/v2/accounts/{id}/addresses/{addr}/transactions` (the v2
  `.../transactions` path 404s for CDP keys) returns `id`, `amount`, `status`
  and `network.hash` — and nothing about origin. So the hash the tester
  supplies is the only link between a transfer and a person, which is why
  `/deposit` requires it. Three consequences are load-bearing: the credited
  amount is always **Coinbase's**, never the claim, so a tester cannot move
  their balance by typing a bigger number (`test_the_venue_amount_wins_over_the_claim`);
  a transfer with no matching claim is recorded and flagged but **never
  apportioned**, because guessing an owner from an amount is how one tester is
  credited with another's money (`/assign <cb_tx_id> <id>` is the admin
  resolution); and arrival **does not verify the wallet**, since money being
  real is not evidence of who sent it — that still needs a chain lookup, and
  it is what gates payouts — now performed by `chain.py` (above).
- **Withdrawals: the venue gives us no safety net, so the flow fails closed.**
  Coinbase **rejects `idem` on sends outright** (`Invalid input parameter.
  Param: Idem`; only the bare `type/to/amount/currency/network` body is
  accepted, `description` is refused too), so there is **no venue-side
  idempotency** — a resend is a second real payment with nothing at the far
  end to collapse it. Consequences, all load-bearing: the ledger debit happens
  at **request** time inside `_write_txn` so two requests cannot spend one
  balance; `mark_withdrawal_submitting` records intent **before** the API call,
  because otherwise a crash mid-call leaves no evidence a payment might exist;
  a `PayoutError.submitted` outcome becomes `unknown`, which **neither refunds
  nor retries** (refunding could hand back money that already left, retrying
  could send it twice) and halts the queue via `pool.halt_payouts` for a human;
  and `watchdog._payout_sweep` sends **one** payout per pass. Approving from
  the admin card only queues — the network call stays out of the Telegram
  callback so a Telegram retry cannot become a second payment.
- **Withdrawals send without an admin tap** (`POOL_AUTO_APPROVE_WITHDRAWALS`,
  on). `request_withdrawal` writes the row straight to `approved`, so
  `_payout_sweep` takes it on the next 60s pass and the money is on-chain in
  roughly a minute. This removes a **human step, not a check**: the halt
  switch, `POOL_PAYOUTS_ENABLED`, the minimum, the per-request and per-user
  and global daily caps, the chain-verified destination via `payout_target`,
  and the available balance read inside `_write_txn` all resolve at request
  time and are unchanged. The admin tap was arbitrating a decision no human
  was making, and its only measurable effect was however long it took someone
  to look at their phone — a tester watching their own money sit still is a
  worse outcome than anything the tap prevented. Admins are still notified,
  but the card carries **no buttons** when auto-approved, because
  `decide_withdrawal` acts only on a `requested` row and offering dead
  controls is worse than offering none. Setting the flag off restores the
  Approve/Deny gate; that is the lever if a payout ever needs a human look.
- **A payout costs more than it sends, and the tester pays it.** Measured:
  a $2.00 Ethereum USDC send debited **$2.148356**, a flat ~$0.148 fee on top.
  Flat, not proportional, hence `POOL_MIN_WITHDRAWAL_USD = 50` (keeps the fee
  under ~0.3%). The tester is debited `amount + fee` so the pool's books stay
  exactly level with the venue; charging only the send amount would quietly
  bleed the house on every withdrawal. `POOL_WITHDRAWAL_FEE_RESERVE_USD = 3.00`
  is held at request time as gas headroom and **trued up and refunded** once
  the real fee is known. `pool.max_withdrawal_usd()` subtracts the reserve, so
  a quoted "max" cannot fail at the moment someone is taking their money out.
- **A payout's progress cannot be read back.** `GET` on a single v2
  transaction 404s exactly like the list endpoint, even with transfer scope,
  so settlement is confirmed by the balance moving or by a chain lookup. That
  makes the chain lookup load-bearing in **two** places now — sender
  attribution for deposits and settlement for withdrawals.
- **Payouts go to the address frozen at request time**, not the current one,
  so approving a wallet change cannot redirect a payout already in flight.
  Caps (`POOL_MAX_WITHDRAWAL_USD`, per-user and global daily) are blast-radius
  limits on a bug or a stolen Telegram account, not product policy.
- **Every balance move runs under `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`.** `_apply_event` reads a
  balance, adds to it in Python, and writes the absolute result, so it is a
  read-modify-write; sqlite starts its implicit transaction at the first
  *write*, not the first read, so `check the balance -> decide -> write` is not
  atomic on a plain connection. `pool._write_txn` takes the write lock before
  the read and every balance-moving path goes through it (`credit`, `debit`,
  `record_intent`, `release_intents`, `expire_stale_intents`, `open_stakes`,
  `book_exit`, `book_close`). Measured, with the guard removed: eight
  simultaneous withdrawals of a $1,000 balance **all** succeeded and left the
  account at −$1,000, ten $150 requests paid out $2,350 against $1,000, and
  ten concurrent $100 credits landed as $500 — five silently lost. `_AVAILABLE_SQL`
  is the single definition of withdrawable cash so the figure quoted to a
  tester and the check inside the transaction cannot drift apart.
- **`pool.withdrawable_usd()` is computed server-side, never accepted from a
  caller**, so a "withdraw max" button cannot quote a number the ledger then
  refuses, and a crafted request cannot name its own ceiling. It is
  `MAX(cash − reserved, 0)`: reserved covers intent budgets and the margin
  behind open stakes, so a withdrawal cannot reach money committed to a live
  position.
- **Nothing that moves money carries a NULL `ref`.** The dedupe index is
  partial (`WHERE ref IS NOT NULL`), so a withdrawal booked with `ref=None` —
  as `debit` used to — sat outside it and could be replayed and charged twice.
  Callers with an operation id pass it (`withdrawal_request:{id}`,
  `cb_deposit:{tx}`); an ad-hoc admin correction has no id and is genuinely
  repeatable, so it gets a unique `adhoc:{admin}:{uuid}` rather than a
  pretence of idempotence. `test_every_balance_event_carries_a_ref` pins it.
- **Idempotence is structural.** Every credit is keyed
  `cb_deposit:{coinbase_tx_id}` against the `pool_events` dedupe index, and
  `pool_chain_deposits` is keyed on Coinbase's own transaction id, so the 60s
  sweep re-reading the same rows is a no-op, a crash between the credit and
  the status write is recovered rather than repeated, and a manual credit
  followed by arrival books once. Transfers already on the address when the
  watcher first runs are recorded as `baseline` — the two sitting there are
  house capital, and treating history as unclaimed tester money would alert on
  both — but a filed claim overrides that, so a deposit landing during the
  first sweep is still credited.
- **Pooled fill model.** A funded tester's Accept records a `pool_intents` row
  reserving `POOL_RISK_PCT` × available cash as their risk budget. At fill
  time (`execute._execute`), pending budgets on the ref (`cycle_id` for HQ,
  `mill_<idea_id>` for mill) add whole extra CDE contracts to the house order;
  the actual fill is split pro-rata to budgets (house included) as
  `pool_stakes`. Budgets too small for a whole contract still buy ownership of
  the aggregate fill — they dilute the house rather than force size. The house
  always fills its own clip; testers only ever add on top.
- **Late = missed.** If the order fired, was replaced, or expired before the
  fill, the watchdog sweep (`watchdog._pool_sweep`) releases the intent and
  DMs the tester. Nothing is ever chased on a tester's behalf.
- **Exits are pro-rata and idempotent.** Every booked exit leg
  (`execute._pool_book_exit`, keyed by venue order id) credits each stake its
  `share_frac` of the leg's P&L and releases margin by the fraction closed;
  `_pool_book_close` finalises. Cash only ever moves by appending a
  `pool_events` row carrying the balance after it, so the journal alone
  reconstructs any account.
- **Reconcile freeze.** Every ~10 min the watchdog checks **whole-account cash**
  covers the sum of tester cash claims (`pool.reconcile`). A shortfall beyond
  `POOL_RECON_TOLERANCE_USD` freezes NEW intents, alerts admins, and never
  adjusts a balance — a human audits `pool_events` against Coinbase and calls
  `pool.unfreeze_intents()`.
- **What counts as backing (`DerivGateway.get_cash_assets`).** Spot USD + USDC
  wallets (`/api/v3/brokerage/accounts`) plus `cfm_usd_balance`. Deliberately
  excluded, all in the under-counting direction, because a floor check that
  over-reports coverage is the one failure that matters: `cbi_usd_balance`
  (a second *view* of the same consumer spot money — counting both double
  counts), spot crypto other than USDC (needs pricing; not what a dollar claim
  is backed by), and `total_pending_transfers_amount` (may have left one pot
  without arriving in the other). Deployability is recorded alongside but never
  gates the check, and takes **two** numbers: `collateral_usd` is what sits in
  CFM ($68.67 on 2026-09-15) while `buying_power_usd` is what the venue will
  actually lend against it ($3,737.92, because Coinbase counts the spot USDC as
  futures collateral at trade time). The first alone reads as a desk out of
  money when it is not. An unreadable, zero, or
  truncated balance **skips** the check; freezing every tester's Accepts on a
  transient API error is worse than checking ten minutes late.

**Later / scaling (not built):** per-tester Coinbase sub-accounts or managed
sub-portfolios would remove the shared-margin coupling and let reconcile match
per-account instead of in aggregate; automated deposit detection (a chain
watcher attributing transfers by sender, with the txid as fallback and a
quarantine for unmatched ones) would retire the manual Credit tap and supply
the `sender` that verifies a wallet; withdrawal requests mirror the deposit

`Open account` creates a one-time personal demo book (not real funding). Trade broadcasts send a **concise decision card** (decision chart + friendly caption with price-move % to TP1/SL, Accept/Reject/See more). Full canonical rationale and structure/entry charts are deferred to **See more**. Rejected/expired users may get one missed-connection Join invite when the house position is ≥ +0.5R.

### The two books are judged on different things

Eva HQ and the mill must not be tuned against the same objective, and results
measured on one do not transfer to the other.

| | Eva HQ | Trade mill |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Capital growth | Subscriber engagement and idea flow |
| Judged on | Expectancy (mean R per trade) | **Hit rate** — directionally right above 50% of the time |
| Cadence | Few trades, abstention-first | Continuous; a day with no cards is a failure |
| Losing trades | Bounded risk is the whole point | May lose more than winners win |
| P&L role | The goal | A constraint — tolerable if negative, not if subscribers notice the bleed |

Hit rate on the mill is bought with **bracket geometry**, not signal quality: a
nearer target and a wider stop raises it monotonically for any entry. The
measured sweep over the recorded book is `trade_ideas/analysis/mill_hitrate.py`,
with the cost floor that rules out the tightest targets in `_cost_overlay.py`.
Full product framing is in `trade_ideas/README.md` → "What the mill is
optimising for".
